# Knowledge-Base Governance

Reduce conflicting copies in a shared knowledge base by making authority, navigation, and change verification explicit.

## Workflow

1. **Inventory.** Documents, owners, audiences, links, and each item's type: policy, procedure, evidence, template, or pointer.
2. **Assign authority.** One authoritative location per rule or fact; mark other copies as pointers and link back. Newest or easiest to find is not authority. When two copies conflict and the owner has not ruled, stop and ask.
3. **Choose an edit method.** For shared or actively edited documents, check revision and collaboration state and make a narrow edit. Replace a whole document only if it is controlled and a prior version is recoverable.
4. **Record the change:** what, why, who approved, which linked copies and indexes need updating.
5. **Read back.** Confirm the intended content is present, not just that the revision advanced. Open important links as the target reader would; compare derived copies with the authority.
6. **Closeout scan** for stale terms, broken links, duplicate rules, and pointers to missing pages. Report open ownership or approval questions; do not invent answers.

## Must-check items

Rules for any document store. Examples illustrate; they do not bound the check.

- **Duplicate pages in one batch.** Name the authoritative one; never delete the others without the owner.
- **One term written several ways.** Search every variant, otherwise stale copies survive the scan.
- **Mixed units, basis or date formats inside a rule.** Unify first, or both the "latest" comparison and the figure mislead.
- **A page status its approval evidence does not support.** Report the mismatch; do not promote or retire the page on your own.

## Examples

Illustrations only — replace them with your own domain's cases.

- Two copies of the same policy, identical except the owner field.
- A term appearing as an abbreviation, in traditional characters, and misspelled.
- A threshold stated in units in one paragraph and in thousands in another.
- A page marked "effective" with no approval record.

## Pitfalls

- **Copy-paste turns a pointer into a second copy** that will drift.
- **Concurrent editing:** a newer revision may be someone else's save. Read back the specific content you changed.
- **Link permission is per reader.** The editor opening a link proves nothing for the audience.
- **Deleted or moved pages** leave pointers to nothing; the scan must actually open them.

## Self-check

- Each rule has one authoritative location; every copy is marked as a pointer.
- Each edit was read back for the specific content, not just the revision number.
- Open ownership or approval questions are listed as open.
